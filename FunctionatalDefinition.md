# Fundamental Analysis Parameter Definitions

This document defines and explains the key fundamental analysis parameters used to screen and rank companies in the Pharmaceutical sector for **StockBenchMark**.

---

## 1. Price-to-Earnings (PE) Ratio
- **Formula**: 
  $$\text{PE Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Share Price}}{\text{Earnings Per Share (EPS)}}$$
- **Explanation**: The PE ratio measures how much investors are willing to pay per rupee of current earnings. A lower PE compared to the industry average or historical trend suggests undervaluation, whereas a high PE indicates high growth expectations or overvaluation. In pharmaceuticals, companies with strong R&D pipelines often command higher PE ratios.

---

## 2. Price-to-Book (PB) Ratio
- **Formula**: 
  $$\text{PB Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Share Price}}{\text{Book Value Per Share}}$$
- **Explanation**: The PB ratio compares a company's market value to its book value (assets minus liabilities). In capital-intensive or asset-heavy parts of the pharma sector (like bulk drug manufacturers/APIs), PB ratio is a key safety metric. A low PB ratio (typically < 3.0) can signal value, provided profitability (ROE) remains high.

---

## 3. Profit After Tax (PAT) & PAT Margin
- **Formula**: 
  $$\text{PAT Margin} = \frac{\text{Profit After Tax}}{\text{Total Revenue}} \times 100$$
- **Explanation**: PAT represents the net profit or bottom line of the company after all operating expenses, interest, depreciation, and taxes have been deducted. PAT growth and PAT margin indicate the pricing power, operational efficiency, and overall profitability of the pharma firm. Higher margins are common for patented or niche formulation players compared to generic manufacturers.

---

## 4. Market Capitalization
- **Formula**: 
  $$\text{Market Cap} = \text{Total Outstanding Shares} \times \text{Current Share Price}$$
- **Explanation**: Market Cap categorizes the company into Large-Cap, Mid-Cap, or Small-Cap. Larger pharma companies (e.g. Sun Pharma, Cipla) generally offer stability, regulatory compliance, and global reach, while mid/small-caps offer higher growth potential alongside higher volatility and regulatory risks.

---

## 5. Dividend Yield
- **Formula**: 
  $$\text{Dividend Yield} = \frac{\text{Annual Dividend Per Share}}{\text{Current Share Price}} \times 100$$
- **Explanation**: The dividend yield shows how much a company pays out in dividends each year relative to its stock price. Stable, mature pharma companies often pay regular dividends, acting as a defensive cushion for investors during market downturns.

---

## 6. Return on Equity (ROE)
- **Formula**: 
  $$\text{ROE} = \frac{\text{Net Income (PAT)}}{\text{Shareholders' Equity}} \times 100$$
- **Explanation**: ROE measures the profitability of a business in relation to the book value of shareholder equity. An ROE above 15% is generally considered strong in the pharma sector, indicating efficient use of equity capital to generate earnings.

---

## 7. Debt-to-Equity (D/E) Ratio
- **Formula**: 
  $$\text{D/E Ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Debt}}{\text{Total Shareholders' Equity}}$$
- **Explanation**: Debt-to-Equity indicates the proportion of debt and equity used to finance the company's assets. Pharma companies require heavy initial investments in R&D and manufacturing plants (USFDA compliant facilities). A lower D/E ratio (typically < 0.5) indicates low financial risk and robust balance sheet strength.
